Franklin ProSensor M90: wider LED display

Franklin specifies nine sensors and nine LEDs for the M90. Its display follows density changes across the sensor area and identifies likely stud edges and center without an initial calibration step. Choosing and using a stud finder

  • Know what is being indicated: an edge finder needs two marks and a midpoint; a center-finding display is an estimate based on its sensor response.
  • Match the wall: published maximum depth is conditional. Thick plaster, tile, foil-backed insulation, moisture and texture can reduce reliability.
  • Scan more than once: check at several heights, from both directions, and look for a consistent vertical pattern. Use plans and visible utility runs as independent clues.
  • Plan the mounting load: selecting an anchor or fastener requires confirming framing material, wall construction and the item's load; a stud indication alone is not a load rating.

Before penetrating the wall

Locate nearby outlets, switches, plumbing and gas lines. Turn off electrical, gas and water supplies as appropriate and confirm conditions with the right professional tools or a qualified tradesperson. A stud finder or built-in AC warning cannot prove that a cavity contains no live wire or pipe. Do not drill based on one reading, especially near utility runs.

Frequently asked questions

Can these tools identify studs through plaster?

Not reliably in every plaster wall. Franklin says depth and irregularity can defeat detection; Zircon recommends its e50 for dry interior walls and notes surface conditions change results. Verify your wall before relying on a scan.

Does a wiring alert mean it is safe to drill if no warning appears?

No. AC indicators can miss live conductors, and the M90 does not provide a dedicated live-wire-clearance result. Use independent checks and shut off utilities before penetrating a wall.
