Which hub should you choose?

Amazon Echo Hub: an Alexa control panel on the wall

Amazon describes Echo Hub as an 8-inch touchscreen for smart home control. It can show compatible camera feeds, operate lights and launch routines. Its built-in hub supports Zigbee, Sidewalk, Thread, Bluetooth and Matter; Amazon says select devices can still be controlled when the internet is down. It can mount on a wall or sit on an optional stand. A compatible PoE adapter is another connection option, not something to assume is included.

Best for: an Alexa household that wants a fixed, visible dashboard. Trade-off: it is Alexa-centered and Amazon does not list native Z-Wave support. Choose a different controller if your existing Z-Wave network is the priority.

Google Nest Hub (2nd gen): a display for Google Home

Google's current US specifications list a 7-inch, 1024 x 600 touchscreen, a built-in Thread border router and Matter support. It works as a hub for Google Home and also includes Soli sensing. This is a display-first choice for managing a Google Home setup, rather than a direct Z-Wave controller. Google lists Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, but does not list Zigbee or Z-Wave radios for this model.

Best for: a household already using Google Home and wanting a compact tabletop screen. Trade-off: no listed Zigbee or Z-Wave radio; verify the path for each existing device and bridge.

Aeotec Smart Home Hub: SmartThings with Z-Wave

Aeotec documents Z-Wave and Zigbee support for its original Smart Home Hub, along with Matter and a Thread border router. It connects to the SmartThings platform and is managed through the mobile app, so there is no room display. Aeotec says some limited automations with some devices work without internet, while app access, cloud services, updates and remote control generally need a connection. That is narrower than claiming all routines run locally.

Best for: buyers with compatible Z-Wave or Zigbee devices who want SmartThings. Trade-off: setup and control depend on the app, and offline behavior varies by device and automation. Aeotec also sells a distinct Smart Home Hub 2; do not assume that model has the original hub's Z-Wave radio.

What matters before purchase

Start with your current devices. A Matter logo can simplify some cross-platform setup, but it does not mean every function of every device is available in every app. Thread is a network technology; a Thread border router does not itself promise compatibility with an arbitrary Zigbee or Z-Wave device.

Choose the control surface. Echo Hub is a fixed Alexa panel, Nest Hub is a Google Home display, and Aeotec relies on the SmartThings phone app. If you want a display for video calling and entertainment, compare current smart displays separately rather than assuming a dedicated hub panel serves that role.

Plan for internet outages. Ask which specific devices and automations need cloud services. Neither Matter nor a local radio guarantees the entire system works offline.

Frequently asked questions

Does Echo Hub work with Z-Wave locks?

Amazon does not list a Z-Wave radio for Echo Hub. A compatible third-party bridge or integration may be needed; verify the specific lock and setup before purchase.

Does Nest Hub (2nd gen) replace a Zigbee bridge?

Google documents Matter and a Thread border router, not a Zigbee radio, for this model. Keep or plan for a compatible bridge when a Zigbee device requires one.
