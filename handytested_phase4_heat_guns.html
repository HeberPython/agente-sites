Wagner HT1000: straightforward heat for occasional projects

Wagner lists 750 F and 1,000 F settings and two fan speeds for its HT1000. The manufacturer describes uses including paint removal and loosening stuck hardware. It is a practical starting point when an intermediate temperature is not essential. Wagner lists specialty nozzles separately, so check the contents of the exact package.

Best for: occasional DIYers wanting simple controls. Trade-off: no variable temperature setting for sensitive materials.

BLACK+DECKER HG1300: a supplied glass-protect nozzle

The HG1300 is another 750 F / 1,000 F corded choice. BLACK+DECKER lists a built-in stand and one glass-protect nozzle in the box, relevant if you work near window trim. DEWALT D26950: more adjustable corded option

DEWALT documents variable temperature control, a kickstand and overload protection. Its D26950 manual lists a 120-1,100 F adjustment range; the product page lists a fishtail surface nozzle and cone nozzle in the box. More adjustment can help when materials call for different settings, but it does not remove the risk of overheating a workpiece.

Best for: regular users who need intermediate settings. Trade-off: more control demands more care; the tool still requires a cord and safe cooldown.

Before using a heat gun

Match the setting to the workpiece, keep the hot nozzle away from skin and combustibles, and follow the tool's cooldown instructions. Paint in older homes can contain lead. The EPA's DIY lead-safe guidance discusses precautions and heat guns below 1,100 F. A low setting is not a substitute for determining whether lead is present or using lead-safe practices.
