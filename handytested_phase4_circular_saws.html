How to choose

  • Start with the battery platform: confirm exact battery compatibility and whether a charger is included.
  • Check the required cut: blade diameter alone is not enough; compare depth at 90 and 45 degrees with the stock you plan to cut.
  • Choose the correct blade: match diameter, arbor, tooth pattern, maximum speed and material to the saw manual.
  • Look beyond RPM: no-load speed cannot establish power, runtime, cut finish or resistance to stalling across different saws.
  • Plan dust control: an adaptor still requires a compatible extractor and does not replace respiratory or eye protection.

Safe setup before every cut

  1. Remove the battery before changing the blade or adjusting the shoe, bevel or depth.
  2. Confirm the lower guard closes freely and the blade is correct, sharp, undamaged and securely installed.
  3. Support the work on both sides of the cut without trapping the kerf, and keep the cord-free work area clear.
  4. Set only enough blade depth for the material, keep both hands in the intended grip positions and stand out of the blade path.
  5. Let the blade reach speed before entering the work and allow it to stop before setting the saw down.

Wear eye and hearing protection and use appropriate dust controls. Kickback can occur when the blade binds or the work shifts. Read the manual for the exact model; controls and guard procedures are not interchangeable.

Frequently asked questions

Is a faster no-load RPM always better?

No. RPM is one specification. Blade, motor behavior under load, material, depth and feed rate all affect the cut.

Does a bare tool include a battery?

Not here. DCS565B and XSS02Z exclude battery and charger; CR6413B-11 is a kit. Verify the seller's exact package before ordering.
