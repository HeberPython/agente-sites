How to choose

  • Choose the task first: fine engraving and polishing need control; repeated cutting and grinding require a tool and accessory rated for that workload.
  • Verify the package: similar names can contain different attachments, guides, accessory counts and storage.
  • Match every accessory: shank size, collet or chuck, rated speed and material compatibility must all fit.
  • Treat included counts carefully: more pieces do not establish grit life, cut quality or suitability.
  • Plan dust and spark control: material removal can create hazardous particles, fragments and heat.

Safe operating checklist

  1. Unplug the tool before changing an accessory, collet or attachment.
  2. Inspect the accessory, seat its shank securely and confirm its rated speed exceeds the selected tool speed.
  3. Wear eye protection and material-appropriate respiratory, hearing and face protection.
  4. Start with the accessory away from your face and workpiece; allow it to reach speed and check for vibration.
  5. Practice on scrap, use light pressure and let the accessory do the work. Stop if the tool, accessory or material overheats.

Cutoff wheels and wire accessories can fragment. Secure the work, keep bystanders away and follow the exact tool and accessory manuals. A rotary tool is high speed and low torque; forcing it increases loss-of-control and accessory-failure risk.
