Non-contact voltage testers can help locate the possible presence of AC voltage before further investigation. They do not measure voltage, detect every hazard or establish that a circuit is safe to touch. Which detector fits your task?

Klein Tools NCVT-3P: two modes and a light

Klein specifies 70-1,000 V AC in standard mode and 12-1,000 V AC in its more sensitive mode. It gives audible and visual indications, a flashlight that can work independently of the detector, auto power-off and an IP67 rating. Its manual lists conditions that affect detection, including tip distance and the user's connection to ground. A no-alert result is not proof of a dead wire.

Best for: a user who needs two AC sensing ranges and an integrated work light. Trade-off: the more sensitive mode may respond to nearby sources; understand the manual before interpreting an indication.

Fluke 1AC-A1-II: standard AC range

Fluke identifies the US 1AC-A1-II variant and lists a nominal 90-1,000 V AC range. A red tip and optional beeper signal detected voltage; a continuous visual indication shows that the unit is on. Fluke also sells a distinct low-voltage 1LAC model, so do not assume this 1AC variant covers 12 V controls or DC circuits.

Best for: preliminary detection on standard AC wiring within the specified range. Trade-off: not intended for lower-voltage AC work; verify the exact variant in the offer.

Greenlee GT-16: adjustable sensitivity

Greenlee's current GT-16 page lists an adjustable detector rated for 5-1,000 V AC, visual and audible indication, low-battery indication and one included AAA battery. Electrical safety first

Do not use a non-contact pen as your sole proof of de-energization. These tools detect an AC field under particular conditions; no alert can occur for reasons other than a dead circuit. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, check function on a known live source before and after use, and use properly rated contact testing and qualified electrical procedures when verifying absence of voltage. Fluke's absence-of-voltage guidance explains the distinction. If you are not qualified to assess the circuit, stop and hire a licensed electrician. Never open a live panel or touch exposed conductors based on a pen's indication.

Frequently asked questions

Can a non-contact tester confirm that power is off?

No. It is a preliminary presence indicator, not an absence-of-voltage verification instrument.

Will these detect DC?

No. The cited sensing specifications concern AC voltage. Choose a properly rated instrument and procedure for DC testing.

Can I compare the lowest voltage numbers directly?

Only as published sensitivity ranges. Real detection depends on setup and conditions described in each manual; a lower minimum does not prove a safer or more accurate device.
