Which sprayer is worth considering?

Wagner FLEXiO 590: nozzle choice for varied surfaces

Wagner's manual identifies an iSpray nozzle for broader surfaces and a Detail Finish nozzle for finer work with thinner materials. It also documents variable air control. That combination may suit a buyer moving between walls and smaller finish projects, provided the coating and nozzle are compatible. The manual calls for proper ventilation, protective equipment and a grounded electrical connection. Do not assume every coating can be sprayed without preparation.

Trade-off: More controls and two nozzles mean more setup and cleaning decisions. Review the manual's material preparation and cleanup sections before choosing it.

Graco TrueCoat 360 Dual Speed (26D281): small-project airless option

Graco lists the 26D281 as a corded handheld airless sprayer with low and high speed settings, FlexLiner paint bags and included paint and stain tips. Before you spray

Read the specific model's manual and the coating label. Wagner's manual warns about ventilation, ignition sources, electrical grounding and protective equipment; Graco likewise provides model-specific operating instructions. Mask the work area and practice on scrap material before the actual surface. For a larger job, consider whether a different sprayer class and capacity would fit better.
